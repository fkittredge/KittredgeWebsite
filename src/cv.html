Fletcher Kittredge

Builder and operator of community-scale infrastructure

fletcher@kittredge.com · LinkedIn · kittredge.com · Biddeford, Maine

Fletcher Kittredge spent thirty years building and running network infrastructure in Maine under tight capital: he founded the state's largest independent internet service provider and led it through five changes of business model, each financed from operations, bank loans, and grants, and he led the effort that won $25 million of federal funding for rural fiber. He worked on the original Internet at BBN, holds a Harvard master's in computer science, and has served on Maine's technology and public-interest boards for two decades.

He now applies that record to AI in Maine: as fractional CEO of MOCSI, the Maine Open Compute Services Initiative, and as president of the Maine AI Coalition.

Experience

1994–2025
Founder and CEO, GWI (Biddeford Internet Corporation), Biddeford, Maine
Founded Maine's oldest internet service provider with $44,000 of initial capital and led it for thirty-one years, to its position as the state's largest independent provider and the first B Corp certified broadband carrier in the United States. Over that time the company changed its business model five times as the technology changed, each time with existing resources:
  • 1994–1998, start-up. Dial-up access in southern Maine; word-of-mouth marketing; founders' capital.
  • 1998–2004, rapid growth. Statewide dial-up, cable broadband, and early DSL; broadcast advertising; local bank loans.
  • 2004–2011, regulated utility. A Class 5 telephone switch and DSL as a competitive local exchange carrier in Maine and southern New Hampshire; statewide bank and family loans.
  • 2011–2020, business fiber provider. Fiber connections over leased and built dark fiber, hosted PBX, a dedicated business sales force; grants, bank loans, and personal loans.
  • 2020–2025, infrastructure builder and operator. Constructed fiber across Maine, New Hampshire, and Vermont.
1993–1994
Consultant, Fidelity Investments

1984–1993
Bolt Beranek and Newman (BBN), Cambridge, Massachusetts
Joined the year after the first rollout of IP to the ARPAnet, on the team that developed the first deployment of the Internet protocols. Source code analyst, then Unix system administrator and C programmer; transferred to the Software Products division in 1988.
